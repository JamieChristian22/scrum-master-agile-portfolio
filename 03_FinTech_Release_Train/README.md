# 🎯 PI Objective Framework

Each PI Objective contains:

- Business outcome
- Responsible team
- Business value
- Dependencies
- Compliance requirements
- Technical constraints
- Risks
- Success criteria
- Status

### Example

**Objective:** Modernize payment-processing capabilities.

**Business Outcome:** Improve transaction reliability and payment-processing stability.

**Success Measure:** Increase release stability while reducing payment-processing incidents.

**Dependencies:** API teams, DevOps, security, compliance, QA.

**Risk:** Shared release specialists and regulatory approval windows.

- Compliance readiness
- Resource availability

# ⚠️ ROAM Risk Management

PI-level risks are reviewed using the SAFe ROAM approach.

### Resolved

The risk is eliminated.

### Owned

An accountable owner is assigned.

### Accepted

The team understands and accepts the exposure.

### Mitigated

Actions reduce likelihood or impact.

# 📥 Definition of Ready

A backlog item is sufficiently ready when:

- Business value is clear
- Customer or operational outcome is understood
- Acceptance criteria are testable
- Regulatory considerations are identified
- Compliance requirements are understood
- Security requirements are identified
- Dependencies are known
- Required designs are available
- Major unknowns are addressed
- Story is sufficiently small
- Team understands the work
- Testing approach is understood

The Definition of Ready is used as a team quality aid rather than a rigid process gate.

---

# ✅ Definition of Done

Work is Done when:

- Acceptance criteria are met
- Development is complete
- Peer review is complete
- Automated tests pass
- Functional testing is complete
- Regression testing is complete where required
- Critical defects are resolved
- Security checks are complete
- Compliance requirements are satisfied
- Documentation is updated
- Deployment requirements are met
- Agreed team quality standards are satisfied

# 🧠 Key Lessons Learned

## 1. Compliance Should Be Shifted Left

Waiting until release readiness to involve compliance creates avoidable delays.

Regulatory requirements should be visible during refinement and planning.

---

## 2. PI Predictability Depends on Team-Level Stability

A Program Increment cannot be predictable when individual teams repeatedly carry unfinished work.

---

## 3. Dependencies Need Active Ownership

Identifying dependencies during PI Planning is not enough.

They require owners, dates, aging visibility, and escalation paths.

---

## 4. Release Stability Requires Evidence

A release date should not determine readiness.

Quality, security, compliance, dependencies, and operational readiness should determine release decisions.

---

## 5. Velocity Alone Does Not Explain Delivery Performance

Flow metrics, carryover, blocked time, predictability, quality, and release stability provide a more useful system view.

---

## 6. Retrospectives Need Measurable Experiments

Improvement actions require owners, success metrics, and follow-through.

---

## 7. Cross-Team Problems Require Cross-Team Governance

Some blockers cannot be solved within a single Scrum team.

Scrum Masters should escalate appropriately through Scrum of Scrums, RTE, architecture, compliance, or leadership.

---

## 8. Team Health Supports Sustainable Delivery

Trust, autonomy, manageable workload, and psychological safety influence delivery reliability.

---

## 9. Scrum Masters Coach Toward Independence

The long-term objective is stronger team self-management rather than permanent dependence on the Scrum Master.

---

## 10. Continuous Improvement Is Never Finished

A successful Agile transformation creates a system that continues identifying and solving new delivery constraints.
