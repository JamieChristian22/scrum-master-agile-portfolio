## 📌 Project Overview

The **Healthcare SAFe Recovery** project is a realistic enterprise Agile delivery simulation focused on stabilizing and improving a large-scale SAFe environment supporting healthcare technology products and operational platforms.

- Electronic Medical Records (EMR)
- Patient engagement systems

# 🎯 PI Objectives Framework

Each PI Objective includes:

- Business outcome
- Responsible team
- Business value
- Dependencies
- Risks
- Success criteria
- Current status

### Example

**Success Measure:** Increase workflow completion reliability while reducing manual exceptions.

**Dependencies:** Integration APIs, QA environment, configuration support.

**Risk:** Shared engineering resources.

# ⚠️ ROAM Risk Management

Program Increment risks are categorized using the SAFe ROAM model.

### ROAM

**Resolved**

Risk is no longer relevant.

**Owned**

A named individual or team owns the response.

**Accepted**

The risk is understood and accepted.

**Mitigated**

Actions reduce probability or impact.

# 📥 Definition of Ready

A backlog item is considered sufficiently ready when:

- Business value is understood
- Patient or user outcome is clear
- Acceptance criteria are testable
- Compliance considerations are understood
- Dependencies are identified
- Required data is available
- Architecture questions are addressed
- Story is sufficiently small
- Team understands the work
- Testing approach is understood
- Work can reasonably be completed during the sprint

The Definition of Ready is used as a **team quality aid rather than a rigid Scrum gate**.

---

# ✅ Definition of Done

Work is considered Done when:

- Acceptance criteria are satisfied
- Development is complete
- Peer review is complete
- Automated tests pass
- Functional testing is complete
- Regression testing is complete where required
- Critical defects are resolved
- Documentation is updated
- Security requirements are addressed
- Compliance requirements are satisfied
- Deployment requirements are complete
- Team quality standards are met

# 🧠 Key Lessons Learned

## 1. PI Predictability Depends on Sprint Health

Program Increment delivery becomes unreliable when individual teams consistently carry unfinished work.

---

## 2. Dependencies Must Be Managed Before PI Execution

Dependency identification during PI Planning is not enough.

Dependencies require active ownership throughout the Program Increment.

---

## 3. Retrospectives Need Measurable Follow-Through

Improvement ideas require owners, success measures, and validation.

---

## 4. Healthcare Quality Cannot Be Deferred

Testing, security, compliance, and operational readiness must be integrated into delivery.

---

## 5. ART-Level Problems Require System-Level Responses

Cross-team blockers often cannot be solved by an individual delivery team.

The Scrum Master must know when to escalate through Scrum of Scrums, RTE, architecture, or leadership channels.

---

## 6. PI Planning Is a Planning Event, Not a Guarantee

Plans must adapt when new risks, dependencies, and information emerge.

---

## 7. Metrics Should Improve Learning

Velocity and predictability metrics should support better planning and conversation rather than individual performance evaluation.

---

## 8. Team Health Affects Delivery Reliability

Psychological safety, workload, collaboration, and autonomy influence sustainable performance.

---

## 9. Scrum Masters Should Coach Toward Independence

The goal is increasingly self-managing teams, not permanent dependence on the Scrum Master.

---

## 10. Agile Recovery Requires Continuous Improvement

Recovery is not complete when metrics improve once.

Teams need systems that continue identifying and solving new delivery constraints.
