# Facilitation Playbook

## Daily Scrum becomes status reporting
**Signal:** Team members report to the Scrum Master instead of planning with each other.  
**Intervention:** Re-anchor on the Sprint Goal, work flow, blockers and the next 24 hours; rotate facilitation.  
**Success:** Developers coordinate directly and leave with a shared plan.

## One voice dominates
Use silent writing, round-robin input, or 1-2-4-All before open discussion. Expertise remains valued, but participation is broadened.

## Team is silent
Use anonymous input, smaller pairs, specific prompts and blameless framing. Silence is treated as a facilitation signal, not consent.

## Product Owner pushes mid-sprint scope
Return to the Sprint Goal. Ask what will be removed, whether the goal changes, and what tradeoff is being made.

## Sprint Goal is missed
Inspect planning assumptions, dependencies, interruption, goal clarity and daily decision-making. Do not hide a missed goal behind high story completion.

## Retrospective repeats the same problem
Turn the issue into a small experiment with owner, measure and review date. Escalate systemic blockers beyond team authority.

## Conflict becomes personal
Restate observable facts, impact and shared outcome; use structured turn-taking and explicit decision criteria.

## Leadership misuses velocity
Explain that story points are team-relative planning data. Redirect to predictability, outcomes, cycle time, quality and value. Never rank individuals by velocity.
