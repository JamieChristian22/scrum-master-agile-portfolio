# 🔄 Retrospective Experiment Framework

Retrospectives are treated as improvement engines rather than discussion meetings.

Each improvement action follows:

**Observed Problem**

↓

**Root Cause**

↓

**Improvement Hypothesis**

↓

**Experiment**

↓

**Owner**

↓

**Success Metric**

↓

**Next-Sprint Measurement**

↓

**Adopt / Adjust / Stop**

### Example

**Problem:** Excessive sprint carryover

**Hypothesis:** Stories are entering sprints before dependencies and acceptance criteria are sufficiently clear.

**Experiment:** Apply strengthened Definition of Ready criteria during refinement.

**Success Metric:** Reduce carryover below 10%.

**Result:** Carryover trend decreases.

**Decision:** Incorporate the practice into the team's standard refinement process.

# 📥 Definition of Ready

The portfolio uses a practical Definition of Ready to improve backlog quality.

A story is considered ready when:

* Business value is understood
* User outcome is clear
* Acceptance criteria exist
* Dependencies are identified
* Major unknowns are resolved
* Story is sufficiently small
* Team understands the work
* Required designs or inputs are available
* Story can reasonably be completed within the sprint

The Definition of Ready is used as a **team quality aid**, not a rigid Scrum gate.

---

# ✅ Definition of Done

Work is considered Done only when agreed quality expectations are satisfied.

Typical criteria include:

* Acceptance criteria met
* Development complete
* Peer review complete
* Testing complete
* Critical defects resolved
* Documentation updated
* Security requirements addressed
* Product Owner acceptance completed where applicable
* Deployment requirements satisfied
* Work meets agreed team quality standards

This reduces incomplete work being counted as finished.

# 📦 Carryover Root-Cause Analysis

Carryover is analyzed instead of treated as a simple performance failure.

Common causes evaluated include:

* Oversized stories
* Hidden dependencies
* Late scope changes
* Insufficient refinement
* External blockers
* Testing bottlenecks
* Production support interruptions
* Capacity assumptions
* Technical uncertainty

Corrective actions target the **cause of carryover**, not the team.

# 🧰 Facilitation Playbook

The portfolio includes approaches for common facilitation challenges.

### Silent Team

Use round-robin input, anonymous brainstorming, and targeted open questions.

### Dominant Voice

Use structured turn-taking and independent idea generation before discussion.

### Sprint Scope Pressure

Return discussion to Sprint Goal, capacity, tradeoffs, and Product Owner decision-making.

### Recurring Blockers

Move from individual incident resolution to systemic root-cause analysis.

### Retrospective Fatigue

Rotate formats and focus retrospectives on one meaningful improvement experiment.

### Conflict

Separate people from the problem, clarify facts and interests, and facilitate shared options.

### Missed Sprint Goal

Analyze system conditions, assumptions, dependencies, and learning rather than assigning blame.

# 🧠 Scrum Master Philosophy

My Scrum Master approach is based on several principles.

### 1. Protect the Sprint Goal, Not a Task List

The purpose of a sprint is to achieve a meaningful outcome.

### 2. Remove Systems Problems, Not Just Individual Blockers

Recurring impediments often indicate process, dependency, organizational, or technical-system problems.

### 3. Metrics Should Create Conversations

Velocity and flow metrics should help teams learn rather than become individual productivity targets.

### 4. Retrospectives Need Experiments

Improvement ideas should have owners, success measures, and follow-through.

### 5. Healthy Teams Deliver More Sustainably

Trust, psychological safety, focus, autonomy, and manageable workload are important delivery conditions.

### 6. Scrum Masters Coach Rather Than Command

The goal is stronger team self-management, not dependence on the Scrum Master.

### 7. Transparency Enables Adaptation

Delivery problems become easier to solve when teams and stakeholders share an accurate view of reality.
